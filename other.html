Other people's art

With the development of social media platforms and streaming services, my skills in navigating cool stuff online have deteriorated. Perhaps, you feel the same way. I believe, it happened because the culture of sharing and adoring art has been replaced by the habit of consuming the infinite fields of algorithmically generated feed. I want to reintroduce this culture, to the extent that I can, and for this reason I am going to collect here lesser-known sonic and visual artists that I really like and that have been an inspiration for me. This list is by no means exhaustive, and I am going to try to update it from time to time
  • Sayaka Botanic is a violinist and a one-person orchestra. She creates captivating thick sound textures out of her instrument and effect pedals
  • Nikita Lisitsa is an indie game developer. He runs a blog where he talks about gamedev, graphics, and the relevant mathematics
  • Cat Liu is a printmaker and a media designer. For the purposes of studying indigo dye making, Cat built a monitoring system for the indigo vat installed in Bioclub Tokyo, that can be sonified
  • Artyom Markaryan (ITSU) is an electronic musician and Saori Ueda (ARS) is a visual artist. In their joint project they use systems of triggers and sidechains to create jagged rhythms and glitchy structures, synchronously sonic and visual
  • Dana Ollestad is a multi-media artist. He uses light-sensitive pickups to allow us to listen to Flashes of Light We Can't See. The live performances can be very bright, so sunglasses are recommended
  • Arpon Raksit, on top of being a mathematician, is a composer and a videographer. He records videos of places and solidifies their atmosphere in the form of a audiovisual poems
  • Katya Zeynetdinova is a violinist, who happens to be a part of the coolest projects happening in Europe. Two examples are the chamber orchestra rendering of Aphex Twin and the instrumental rendering of O'Neill's play Long Day's Journey Into Night
